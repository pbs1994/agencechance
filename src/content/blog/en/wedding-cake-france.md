The wedding cake in France often surprises international couples. Many picture a snow-white tiered cake, just like in the movies. Then, at the caterer’s tasting, they are offered a tower of cream puffs.

It is neither a mistake nor a cost-cutting measure. It is a French tradition.

In France, “wedding cake” can mean very different desserts. What you have in mind shapes the planning, the budget and the choice of vendors.

We organise international weddings in France and regularly hear the same question: “But where is the cake?” So we have gathered everything you need to know in one article.

You will learn which options exist, how they differ and how to choose the one that suits you.

## The wedding cake in France: a broad notion

### The cream puff tower

The most traditional French wedding dessert is called the pièce montée, or croquembouche. Most often it is a tall pyramid of small cream puffs (choux).

The choux are filled with pastry cream, held together with caramel and stacked into a cone. The base is often made of nougatine. The tower is decorated with sugared almonds, flowers and spun caramel.

It is a spectacular dessert. It is often brought out to music and sparklers. Guests stand, applaud and take photos. For many French families, it is the very symbol of the wedding dinner.

We cover other customs in our article on [French wedding traditions](/en/blog/information/french-wedding-traditions/).

### The macaron tower

The second popular option is a tower of macarons. It looks lighter and more contemporary. Incidentally, the [macaron](https://www.france.fr/ru/article/obekt-zhelania-makaron/) was not born in Paris but in Nancy, in Lorraine.

Macarons can be matched to the colours of your décor: pastel shades, white and gold, bold accents. Flavours can be combined too: vanilla, raspberry, pistachio, chocolate, lemon.

Couples who love French classics but want a more delicate presentation often choose this tower.

### Other dessert options

The tower is not the only option. Caterers can suggest other desserts.

For example, a dessert table with mini pastries, tarts, mousses and fruit, where guests help themselves. It is practical and beautiful, especially at an outdoor summer wedding.

Sometimes the caterer also offers a simple cake, usually one or two tiers with classic decoration. It is a good compromise if the cake-cutting moment matters to you.

### Dessert is already on the menu

An important point: in France, the wedding dessert is usually part of the caterer’s offer.

When you choose your menu, it already includes a cream puff tower, macarons or another dessert. The caterer makes it, brings it and handles the service.

You don’t need to find a separate pastry chef or think about delivery, storage and cutting. It is all part of the team serving your dinner.

You can often taste the dessert in advance at the menu tasting, and discuss fillings and decoration there.

We explain how catering works at countryside venues in our article on a [wedding in a French château](/en/blog/information/wedding-in-french-chateau/).

### How much per guest

If you choose this option, it really is simple.

Plan for about two pieces per guest. For 80 guests, that is around 160 cream puffs or macarons.

Your caterer will confirm the exact quantity, taking into account the format of the evening, other desserts and the serving time. They take care of the rest.

### How dessert is served at a French wedding

A French wedding dinner is long: starters, main course, cheese. Dessert is usually served late in the evening, sometimes close to midnight.

This moment often becomes a highlight of the programme. The lights dim, music plays, the waiters bring out the tower. The newlyweds meet it in the middle of the room, then raise a glass of champagne with their guests.

The dessert is then shared out and the evening flows into dancing.

Coordinate this moment in advance with your host, photographer and caterer, so it looks beautiful and runs without pauses.

## The wedding cake as a work of pastry art

### The tiered cake is a speciality of its own

The classic tiered wedding cake is less common in France, but you can certainly order one.

One thing matters: such a cake is not just a big dessert. It is a distinct branch of pastry art.

The structure must be engineered so the tiers hold. Fillings must keep their shape and flavour. The decoration must be designed: sugar flowers, fresh flowers, hand painting, textured cream.

All of this requires the experience and skill of a specialised pastry chef.

### Why international couples choose it

Tiered cakes are especially popular at international and multicultural weddings.

For many guests from Russia, Ukraine, Kazakhstan, the US or the UK, a wedding without a cake feels incomplete. Cutting the cake together is as important to them as the first dance.

A French cream puff tower can be a delightful discovery. But sometimes a couple wants to keep their own tradition too — and that is entirely possible.

### Ordered separately from the caterer

A tiered cake is usually ordered in addition to the wedding menu, independently of the caterer.

That gives you freedom: you choose the pastry chef, style and flavour separately. But it also raises new questions.

Who delivers the cake to the venue? Where is it stored until it is served? Is there a fridge large enough? Who cuts and serves it?

In summer this matters even more. French heat can ruin even a beautiful cake if it stands too long without refrigeration.

Also check the venue’s and caterer’s terms. Some charge a cake-cutting fee for an outside cake. Better to know in advance.

### Who can make the cake

There are two main options.

The first is a pâtisserie in your town or in the region where the wedding takes place. France has many excellent pastry shops, and some make celebration cakes to order.

The second is a cake designer who works exclusively on wedding cakes. These pastry chefs work by appointment, often take a limited number of orders and can come to the venue for the final assembly.

Both are possible, but the results will differ.

### What drives the price

The price of a bespoke cake depends on several factors: the number of servings, the number of tiers, the complexity of the decoration (handmade sugar flowers take many hours), the fillings and ingredients, delivery and on-site assembly.

If the cake is only for the ceremonial moment and the main dessert is already on the menu, it can be smaller — which noticeably reduces the budget.

## How to choose a pastry chef

### The narrower the speciality, the better the result

This is the golden rule. The more specialised the pastry chef, the more likely you are to get exactly what you imagined.

A pâtisserie can bake wonderful pastries and tarts. But a tiered wedding cake requires other skills: structure, transport, on-site assembly, complex decoration.

A chef who makes only wedding cakes solves these problems every week. They know which fillings bear the weight of the tiers, which cream won’t melt in two hours, and how to drive a cake along a country road to a château.

So the look, design and taste will be closer to your expectations.

### What to look for

Before ordering, look at the chef’s portfolio. Pay attention not only to beautiful photos but to the range of work. Are there cakes in a similar style? Are there wedding cakes, not just birthday cakes?

Ask about a tasting. Many wedding cake makers let you try several fillings before you decide.

Check the logistics. Who delivers the cake? Is on-site assembly included? How long can the cake stand before it is served?

Think about your guests. Do any have allergies or special diets? A good pastry chef will suggest a solution, such as a gluten-free or nut-free tier.

### When to order

Don’t leave choosing your pastry chef to the last minute.

In high season, from May to September, popular wedding cake makers are booked well ahead, sometimes months in advance. The sooner you settle on a style and a date, the more choice you have.

A good order of play: first choose the venue and caterer, then decide whether you need a separate cake, and only then look for a pastry chef.

### A wedding planner’s help

Finding a good wedding cake maker in France on your own is not easy, especially if you live abroad or don’t speak French.

Wedding planners usually already know trusted pastry chefs who specialise in wedding cakes. They have seen their work at real weddings and know their strengths, lead times and terms.

The planner also coordinates the cake delivery with the venue and caterer — one of those invisible details guests never think about, but on which the perfect serving moment depends.

## The wedding cake is a matter of personal taste

There is no right or wrong choice. A wedding cake in France can take many forms, and each has its charm.

### If you value simplicity

Choose the dessert your caterer offers. A cream puff or macaron tower is beautiful, delicious and traditional.

No extra vendor, no worries about delivery and storage: the team already working at your wedding takes care of everything.

It is a great option for couples who want a truly French wedding and easy planning.

### If a bespoke design matters to you

Order a separate wedding cake from a specialised pastry chef.

This is for you if you want an elaborate composition, signature decoration, a cake in your wedding colours or special flavours. It will become one of the centrepieces of the evening — and a beautiful shot for your photographer.

### You can combine both

Many couples choose a compromise: a small bespoke cake for the cake-cutting moment, and a cream puff or macaron tower from the caterer for the guests.

You keep the French tradition and get the cake of your dreams. It works particularly well at international weddings, where guests from different countries expect different things.

## Frequently asked questions about wedding cakes in France

### Can I bring my own cake to the venue?

Usually yes, but it must be agreed with the venue and caterer in advance. Check whether there is storage space, who will cut the cake and whether there is an extra fee.

### Can I order a cake from a Russian-speaking pastry chef in France?

Yes, there are such chefs, especially in Paris and other large cities. It is convenient if you want familiar flavours such as medovik (honey cake) or Napoleon. Just make sure the chef has experience with wedding cakes and can deliver to the venue.

### How many tiers do I need?

It depends on the number of guests and whether there is another dessert. If the cake is only for the ceremonial moment, two or three tiers are enough. The pastry chef will help size it to your number of servings.

### Can I order a cake without a tasting?

You can, but we don’t recommend it. A tasting helps you choose the filling and avoid surprises on the day. If you live abroad, plan it during a trip to France, for example together with a venue visit.

## A wedding cake in France for every taste

A wedding cake in France doesn’t necessarily look like the one you are used to — and that is part of its charm.

You can choose a classic cream puff tower, order a tiered cake from a master pastry chef, or combine both.

In France, there is a solution for every taste.
