## When France marries Russia: a thousand-year love story

The bond between France and Russia has deep historical roots. As early as the 11th century, Anne of Kyiv became Queen of France by marrying Henry I — the first French-Russian alliance. The connection grew stronger in the 19th century with Russian emigration to France, as the magazine [Russia Beyond](https://www.rbth.com/) tells so well in its articles on French-Russian relations.

A French-Russian wedding is much more than the union of two people. It is the meeting of two rich cultures, centuries-old traditions and different administrative systems. From the Franco-Russian Alliance of 1892 to today’s cultural exchanges, our two countries share a special relationship that is reflected in modern marriages. Every year, hundreds of couples take the step — and the journey calls for careful preparation.

## Different mindsets: when two cultures of love meet

Attitudes to marriage in France and Russia differ in ways that go well beyond traditions. The French see a wedding as the culmination of a love story, favouring intimacy and authenticity. Russians see it as a major social event involving the extended family, colleagues and friends.

The difference shows especially in:

-   **Protocol.** In Russia, each stage of the celebration follows a precise order.
-   **The role of families.** Relatives are far more involved in Russian culture.
-   **Emotions.** More reserved on the French side, more expressive on the Russian side.

Our expertise lies in finding the balance between these sensibilities, so that each family feels respected and honoured.

## Where to marry: France or Russia?

Choosing between France and Russia shapes both the experience and the formalities. A wedding in France — in picture-postcard Normandy or in Paris, the City of Light — appeals to romantics from all over the world. The main advantage: simpler paperwork for a Russian partner who plans to settle in France.

A wedding in Russia allows you to fully embrace Orthodox traditions and the country’s legendary hospitality. Russian celebrations are often more lavish and can last several days. However, the marriage must then be transcribed into the French civil register to be recognised in France — a step we know well.

## The essential documents: an administrative puzzle

Preparing documents is the most technical part of a French-Russian wedding. For a marriage in France, the Russian partner needs a certificate of custom (certificat de coutume), a birth certificate, a certificate of celibacy and a valid passport. Russian documents must be apostilled under the Hague Convention and translated by a sworn translator.

For a marriage in Russia, the French partner provides a similar set of documents, also with sworn translation and legalisation. These steps are tedious but essential to the legal validity of your union. We work with trusted sworn translators and know the administrative circuits of both countries, to help you avoid delays or rejected files.

### Matrimonial regime: protecting your union and assets

For binational couples, the choice of matrimonial regime matters a great deal. French law offers several options, including community of acquired property (the default regime), separation of property, or participation in acquired property. Each has different implications for property in both countries, inheritance rights and cross-border taxation.

We recommend discussing these questions early with a notary specialised in private international law — especially if one spouse owns property or investments in Russia.

## FAQ: answers to your most frequent questions

**How far in advance should we start planning a French-Russian wedding?**  
For a wedding in France, we recommend starting the paperwork six to eight months ahead. For Russia, four to six months is usually enough.

**Which cultural differences should we anticipate?**  
Russian traditions favour big celebrations with many guests, sometimes over several days, while French weddings are often more intimate. Toasts, protocol and attentions to the families deserve special care so that both cultures feel at home.

**What is the best time of year for a French-Russian wedding?**  
Avoid the harsh Russian winter and the French school holidays. May–June and September–October offer pleasant weather in both countries and make travel easier for guests.

**What do you offer for guests who don’t speak the same language?**  
We provide bilingual programmes, discreet interpreters for important speeches, and thoughtful seating plans that encourage conversation.
